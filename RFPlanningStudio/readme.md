2. Experiment Analysis
   ##1️⃣ Increase Gateway Height by +5 mDoes maximum LOS distance change? Yes. Increasing the height from 20 m to 25 m increases the theoretical Radio Horizon (Max LOS) from approximately 21.0 km to 22.9 km.Does link feasibility improve significantly? In this specific 4 km link, the improvement is marginal for power but significant for obstacle clearance.Why? Higher mounting points increase the "line-of-sight" distance by overshooting the Earth's curvature and providing a steeper angle to clear ground-level obstacles.
   ##2️⃣ Increase Antenna Gain to 5 dBiHow does the received power curve change? The entire curve shifts upward by 6 dB (a 3 dB increase at both the Transmitter and Receiver).How much additional range is achieved? Depending on the exponent $n$, this 6 dB boost can extend the effective range by roughly 40–60%.Why does antenna gain extend coverage? Antenna gain focuses radiated energy into a narrower beam rather than scattering it isotropically. In the link budget, higher gain directly offsets path loss, allowing the signal to stay above the sensitivity threshold ($-120$ dBm) at greater distances.
   ##3️⃣ Change Environment Exponent ($n$)How does increasing $n$ affect coverage? Coverage drops drastically. At $n=2.0$ (Free Space), the link could span dozens of kilometers; at $n=4.0$ (Dense Urban), the signal may fail before 1 km.Which has a stronger impact on range? The environment change ($n$) has a far more profound impact than antenna gain.Physical Explanation: Gain is a linear addition (a constant offset), but the exponent $n$ represents an exponential decay. In dense environments, signals suffer from multi-path fading and absorption, meaning power is lost to the surroundings much faster than in an open-air environment.
   ##4️⃣ Move Gateway Location (Fresnel Study)Where is Fresnel radius largest? Exactly at the midpoint ($0.5D$) of the link.Why is the midpoint critical? The Fresnel zone is shaped like an ellipsoid; the laws of geometry dictate that the perpendicular distance from the LOS path to the zone boundary is widest halfway between the two antennas.What happens if the clearance rule (60%) is violated? You enter the "sub-path" diffraction region. Even if the physical line-of-sight is not blocked, the obstacle interferes with the phase-coherent signal, causing significant signal attenuation (often 6 dB or more).3. Final ConclusionIn practical wireless deployment, the environment exponent ($n$) has the strongest impact on coverage. While hardware parameters like Tx power and antenna gain provide "fixed" decibel boosts, the environment dictates the rate at which that energy is depleted. A high-gain system in a dense urban area ($n=4$) will still perform significantly worse than a low-power system in an open field ($n=2$). Therefore, site selection and understanding local topography are more critical to link feasibility than simply increasing hardware specifications.
